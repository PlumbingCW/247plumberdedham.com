Emergency sump pump repair in Dedham, MA means a licensed plumber restores the pump that moves groundwater out of the basement, or installs a new one.

The pump died during a storm.

Pumps fail when you need them most, since a pump that sits idle through a dry spell gets its hardest work in a downpour. The usual culprits are a float stuck against the pit wall, a tripped breaker, a pump burned out from running dry or non-stop, and a clogged intake.

Quick checks before you call:

  1. Is the pump plugged in and is the outlet's GFCI button popped? Reset it once..
  2. Lift the float by hand. If the pump starts, the float is the problem..
  3. If the pump hums but does not move water, the check valve or the impeller is likely at fault..
  4. If the power is out in the neighborhood, the pump has no power and a battery backup matters..

Do not reach into a pit with the pump plugged in. Unplug first.

Where sump pump water must not go.

The Town states it is illegal to discharge clean rain or groundwater into the sanitary sewer. The Town also ran a door-to-door program in 2024 and 2025 in Manor, Oakdale and Greenlodge, inspecting properties for sump pump and roof drain connections to the sanitary sewer after sewage backups were reported in homes.

The Town's sewer bylaw is Chapter 229, and its enforcement section allows civil penalties up to $5,000 per violation, according to the Town's published summary of the bylaw.

For you, the rule is plain: sump discharge must not go to the sanitary sewer. We found no Dedham bylaw naming sump pumps specifically, so we do not cite one. When we replace a pump, we look at where the discharge pipe ends and tell you if it connects to a sewer line.

Outside the house, the pipe should end well away from the foundation on your own lot, and ask the Town if you are unsure.

A plumber can only replace or add what the pit and the discharge route allow. Ask whether the plumber brings a check valve, whether the discharge can be extended away from the foundation, and where the alarm sits.

How a plumber finds the fault.

The diagnosis starts with power, the float and the check valve, in that order, because that is where most failures sit. Next comes a bucket test: pour in water and watch the cycle. A pump that cycles every few seconds on a dry week suggests a faulty check valve letting water flow back, or a high water table.

We cannot see underground, so a pump that runs constantly may point to a broader drainage problem. For that, see basement waterproofing.

Battery backup and the next outage.

Storms that flood basements often knock out power, which is the exact moment a plain electric pump stops. A battery backup pump sits beside the main pump in the pit with its own float and battery. Ask for a unit with an alarm, since a quiet backup that drained its battery is no help. Batteries need testing every few months and replacement on a schedule your plumber can tell you.

Freeze and winter tips.

The January normal low in the Boston area is 23.1 F (NOAA 1991-2020, Boston Logan), so the discharge pipe outside can freeze solid and force the water back into the pit. Keep the outlet clear of snow and ice, and ask about a freeze-resistant outlet. For a cracked pipe, see burst pipe repair.
