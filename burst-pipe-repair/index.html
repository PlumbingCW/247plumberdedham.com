A burst pipe is a split or crack in a water line that lets pressurized water pour into a wall, floor, or basement. Burst pipe repair in Dedham, MA means stopping the flow, then cutting out the damaged section and replacing it.

Our 24/7 emergency dispatch is available at night and on weekends. While you wait, close the main shutoff valve, open the lowest faucet, and turn off the breaker for any wet room.

The first ten minutes after a pipe bursts.

  1. Close the main shutoff valve, usually where the line enters near the meter or the basement wall..
  2. Open the lowest faucet in the house and flush upstairs toilets once to drain the lines..
  3. Turn off the water heater so the element or burner does not run dry..
  4. Cut power to wet rooms at the breaker panel if you can reach it without stepping in water..
  5. Photograph the damage, then start moving belongings and soaking up water..

If the main valve will not turn or the break is outside the house before the meter, call the Dedham-Westwood Water District's 24/7 line at 781-326-1250. The District supplies water in Dedham and can address a problem on its side. Then call us for the part inside your walls.

Why pipes split in a Boston-area winter.

Water expands when it freezes, and the pressure builds between the ice plug and a closed faucet. The pipe usually splits at the weak spot, not where the ice sits. That is why a burst often shows up hours after a cold night, when the ice melts and flow resumes.

NOAA's 1991 to 2020 normals for Boston Logan Airport put the January normal low at 23.1 F, with February at 24.6 F and December at 29.2 F. That is a Boston-area figure, not a Dedham station reading, but it tells you an average night sits below freezing for months. Cold snaps run well below the normal, so exterior walls, garages, and crawlspaces deserve attention before they get there.

Frozen but not burst yet: thawing safely.

If a faucet runs a trickle or nothing and a pipe is frosty or bulging, thaw it slowly. Open the faucet so melting water has somewhere to go. Warm the pipe with a hair dryer or a heating pad, starting near the faucet and working back toward the frozen spot.

Never use a torch or open flame. It can ignite framing and crack the pipe. If you cannot find the frozen section, or you see water leaking as it thaws, shut the main and call. A plumber can thaw the line and check it for hairline cracks that only leak once it is under pressure again.

Buried lines and the 48 inch frost depth.

The Massachusetts residential building code, 780 CMR, lists a frost depth of 48 inches for design. For buried water lines, that means the pipe belongs below that depth so that ground frost does not reach it. A line installed shallower, or one that lost soil cover to grading or erosion, is the one to worry about.

This matters when you replace a service line or add an outdoor spigot line. Ask the plumber how deep the trench will be and whether the depth meets the local code. A frozen buried line often shows up as no water at all, so check with neighbors before assuming the problem is inside your house.

Cracked, split, or burst: what the damage tells you.

The pipe material and the type of break change the repair. Plumbers read the damage before choosing a fix.

What you see Likely cause Typical repair.
Long split along the pipe Freeze expansion Cut out and replace the section.
Pinhole spray Corrosion or wear Replace a short length, check nearby pipe.
Cracked fitting Stress or a freeze New fitting or short section.
Joint pulled apart Movement or water hammer Rejoin, add support, check pressure.
Broken drain line Settling or impact Replace section, test the run.

Copper pipe repair.

Copper is common in many homes and repairs well. A plumber cuts out the damaged piece, then joins new copper with soldered or pressed fittings. Press fittings need no flame, which helps near wood framing, but they must be rated for the pipe and the pressure.

Ask what type of joint will be used and whether the repair is a section or a patch. A clamp can stop a pinhole quickly, but it is a temporary fix, not a lasting repair. Also ask the plumber to check the pipe on both sides of the cut, because copper that failed in one place is often thin elsewhere.

Drain pipe and other pipe leak repairs.

A cracked drain line behaves differently from a pressurized supply break. It leaks only when water runs down it, so the sign is dampness under a sink or a stain below a bathroom after a shower. The repair is a cut-out and a new run of pipe with proper slope. Our leak detection page covers how to track the slow ones down.

Repair now or replace the run?.

A single freeze split in a pipe that is otherwise sound calls for a section repair. If the plumber finds pitting, green crust, or flaking rust along the run, replacing more pipe can prevent a second emergency. Winter habits that prevent a repeat.

  • Let a faucet drip on very cold nights if a pipe runs along an outside wall..
  • Open cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls so room heat reaches the pipes..
  • Disconnect hoses and close the interior shutoff to outdoor spigots..
  • Keep the heat on if you travel, even at a lower setting..
  • Know where your main shutoff is and make sure it turns..
