The state residential code lists a 48 inch frost depth, so buried lines are normally set below it. The pipes that split tend to be in exterior walls, crawl spaces and unheated garages. If one does, burst pipe repair begins with the main shutoff, and a no-heat night calls for boiler repair.

Spring is the other peak. We have no dated Norwood flood record to cite, so treat this as regional behavior: snowmelt and rain arrive together, and a sump that sat idle all winter has to work at once.

MWRA water through town-owned pipes.

Norwood buys all its water from the MWRA, drawn from the Quabbin and Wachusett reservoirs, and sends it through more than 120 miles of locally owned pipe maintained by Public Works. The 2023 MWRA community letter says the Town has cleaned and lined several thousand feet of main to address rusty water and improve fire flows, and replaced 24 hydrants and 201 water services.

After main work, sediment can shake loose and show up as brown water or grit in faucet aerators. Run the cold tap at your lowest faucet for a few minutes. If it stays cloudy at one house but not next door, the cause may be inside, and water line repair is worth asking about.

Lead service lines and the 2023 letter.

The same letter says MWRA supplied water has no lead. Lead enters through household solder or service lines, and solder was banned in 1986. Sampling of 20 homes in 2023 gave a 90th percentile of 7.54 ppb against a 15 ppb action level, with a range of 0.15 to 25.7 ppb.

Two homes exceeded and had their lead services replaced with copper, and more than 130 lead service lines have been replaced to date.

If your home predates 1986, ask any plumber who opens a wall what the solder and service line are. The Board of Health offers a voluntary lead tap test at 781-762-1240 ext. 174, and Public Works is at 781-762-1413.

What to do before the plumber reaches your Norwood address.

Close the main and relieve the line.

If a pipe has split, close the main, then open the lowest tap to drain it. Leave heat on in the rest of the house so the break does not spread.

Treat a wet boiler room with care.

Keep power off if the floor is wet. If you smell gas, leave the building, call 911 from outside and call your gas utility. Do not flip switches on the way out.

Clear the area, especially in a shop.

Move stock, furniture and paperwork off the wet floor. In a restaurant, showroom or office, keep customers and staff away from restrooms and floor drains until it is dealt with.

Call the 24/7 line.

Call (617) 752-0573 and say whether the problem is a home or a business. Give the street and what has failed.

Permits at Town Hall on Washington Street.

The Building Department is on the ground floor of Town Hall, Room 8, 566 Washington Street, and enforces the Massachusetts Plumbing and Gas Code. Office hours are Monday to Friday, 8 am to 4 pm, but the plumbing and gas inspector keeps shorter hours, 11 am to 12 pm and 3 to 4 pm. The main number is 781-762-1240.

Ask the plumber how the permit and inspection will fit those windows, so a Friday-night repair does not stall. Permits are applied for through the Town's online link, and only licensed plumbers can pull them.
