East Dedham grew up as a mill village on Mother Brook, the canal the Town dug in 1639 to link the Charles and Neponset rivers. Today it is a working neighborhood of single-family homes, duplexes and multi-family buildings. That mix shapes the plumbing questions here more than anything else, because a leak or a clog often involves more than one household.

What East Dedham is, from the Town's records.

The master plan lists East Dedham as one of Dedham's six neighborhoods, with about 670 acres and roughly 2,200 housing units. It contains the historic East Dedham Square and an area the Town rezoned to support revitalization. A gazetteer entry also lists East Dedham as a populated place inside Dedham.

The plan names workers' housing in single-family, duplex and multi-family form along High, Milton, Colburn, Maverick and Bussey Streets, plus 19th-century workers' cottages. Landmarks include Mill Pond Park on Colburn Street and the Mother Brook Arts and Community Center at 123 High Street. The East Dedham edge borders Boston's Hyde Park and West Roxbury, according to the Town Clerk. No boundary line is published, so this page does not draw one.

Plumbing in duplexes and multi-family buildings.

In a building with more than one household, the question is often whose pipe it is. Water heaters, supply lines and shutoffs may be shared or separate, and a drain stack can serve several floors. A backup in a lower unit may start with a clog upstairs.

These are general plumbing points, not a finding about East Dedham buildings. When you call, say whether you own or rent, which unit is affected and whether neighbors report the same symptom. Tenants should tell the owner, and the owner decides who calls a plumber. A plumber can then trace which line serves which unit before opening anything up.

Mother Brook, stormwater and your basement.

The Mother Brook canal is managed by the state's Department of Conservation and Recreation for flood control. The master plan also says Dedham's public works crew installed stormwater treatment areas on Colburn Street, Sawmill Lane and Avery Street. Those are public stormwater projects. They are not a statement about any house's basement.

If your basement takes on water, work out first whether it is rain coming in, a leaking pipe or a sump that failed. Leak detection tells a pipe leak from groundwater, and sump pump repair covers the pump itself. Either way, clean water must not go into the sanitary sewer.

Codes, standards and sources.

Plumbing work in Massachusetts is governed by 248 CMR 10.00, the Uniform State Plumbing Code, and plumbers are licensed by the Board of State Examiners of Plumbers and Gas Fitters. You can read the state's consumer fact sheet on plumbers and gas fitters before you hire anyone.
