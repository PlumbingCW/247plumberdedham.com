Where Westwood's water causes trouble first.

The Town's 2023 Hazard Mitigation Plan lists specific drainage weak points. It names the Conant Road and Country Lane culvert, Willard Circle, University Avenue, Gay Street, High Street near the fire station, Hartford Street at Warwick Drive and Crystal Hill Terrace, where 11 homes are listed.

If you live on or near one of those, treat a wet basement after heavy rain as a repeat risk, and ask any plumber about a sump pump with a battery backup.

Sewer capacity shows up in the same plan. It flags an undersized sewer trunk line on Gay Street, a Hartford Street main that needs upsizing, and a pump station near Conant Road and Country Lane that flooding may affect.

The Sewer Department runs about 87 miles of gravity sewer and ten pumping stations. A backup that hits several neighbors points at that town network, so call the Department. A backup in one house points at your own line, and sewer line cleaning with a camera pass can confirm it.

What to do before the plumber arrives in Westwood.

Find the main valve first.

Westwood water comes from the Dedham-Westwood Water District, and the shutoff you want is inside the house, often where the line enters the basement. Turn it clockwise until the flow stops. If water is bubbling in the yard or the meter pit is overflowing, the break is outside, and the District's after-hours water emergency line, 781-326-1250, covers the street side.

Cut power to wet areas.

Do not walk through standing water to reach the furnace, panel or outlet strips. Turn power off at the panel only if you can reach it from dry ground, otherwise leave it alone and tell the dispatcher.

Stop feeding the sewer.

If drains gurgle or a floor drain rises after a storm, stop running sinks, showers and washers. Keep children and pets out, since a sewer backup is a health hazard.

Westwood neighborhoods and the pipes behind them.

Pipe age varies house to house, so ask the plumber what material they actually see at your main and heater before they quote. If the tank itself is weeping at the bottom, water heater repair may not be the answer, and an honest plumber will say so.

What the Dedham-Westwood Water District means for your plumbing.

Westwood and Dedham share one supplier. The District draws groundwater from 17 wells, and five are in Westwood: four by the Neponset River near University Avenue and one near Rock Meadow Brook and Dover Road.

About 23% of supply came from MWRA in 2025. Average hardness was 6.0 grains per gallon in the 2025 report, so white scale on fixtures and heater elements is expected. If your water bill climbs and nothing looks wet, leak detection is the next step.

The same report says lead comes mainly from service lines and home plumbing, and one of 31 homes sampled in late 2025 read 101 ppb. Ask the plumber what your service entry is made of while a wall is open. The District also requires an annual test of any home irrigation backflow device, so ask about a retest if a sprinkler line breaks.

Permits for emergency repairs.

The Building Division handles plumbing and gas permits, with applications filed online through ViewpointCloud. Inspections are scheduled by phone at (781) 320-1091, and processing can take up to 30 days. Only licensed plumbers can pull plumbing permits. Replacing fixtures or piping needs one, so ask how a heater swap will be permitted.
