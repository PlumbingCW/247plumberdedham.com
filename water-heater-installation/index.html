Water Heater Installation in Dedham, MA

Moving a heater, changing fuel or adding a unit takes planning. A licensed plumber handles the permit, the code items and the connections.

Water heater installation in Dedham, MA covers putting a new heater where none stood before, changing fuel, relocating a unit or setting a different size, and it is a planned job rather than a panic call. A licensed plumber sets the tank or tankless unit, connects water, gas or power, installs the required valves and discharge pipe, and applies for the permit.

The details depend on your house, so this page starts with the houses and ends with the paperwork.

Dedham homes and the heater that fits them.

Capes, split-levels and ranches.

The Dedham Master Plan describes the Oakdale area as 1950s capes, split-levels and ranch-style homes. Many of these put the heater in a basement or utility closet near a chimney or vent, which shapes what fuel and venting you can choose. Before you price anything, note where the current vent and gas line, if any, leave the room.

Older homes in the mill-worker neighborhoods.

The same plan calls the Mother Brook mill-worker neighborhoods historic housing, and it reports that about 74% of Dedham houses were built before 1978. An older house may have a tight basement, an old chimney or a long run to the nearest drain. Ask the plumber to look at the route before quoting, because the route decides labor.

Condos and rentals.

About 29% of Dedham households rent, so a landlord or association often approves the work. Get written approval for location and fuel changes before the plumber arrives, and ask who holds the permit paperwork afterward.

Fuel and size: choices to settle before the install date.

You can keep the current fuel for the fastest swap, or change it. The right choice depends on what your house already has, not on a general rule, and we make no claims about which fuel dominates in Dedham.

A gas unit needs a gas line and a vent path. An electric unit needs a dedicated circuit with enough capacity in the panel. A hybrid heat pump unit needs space, airflow and a condensate drain.

Relief valve and discharge pipe: what 248 CMR requires.

Under 248 CMR 10.14(6)(f), a water heater that can exceed 212 F needs a temperature and pressure relief valve. Its discharge pipe must be full size, non-ferrous, with no shut-off valve, and end at a fixture or within 12 inches of the floor.

A tank-type heater also needs a vacuum relief valve. When you review a quote, check that it lists the relief valve and discharge pipe and does not leave them as extras, because an inspector will look for them.

Safe waste pan rules for heaters above living space.

The Mass.gov safe waste pan policy applies to water heaters and boilers of 6 gallons or more installed above an occupied space. The pan must be piped indirectly to a trapped fixture or end within 12 inches of the floor. On a replacement, an approved automatic shut-off device plus a pan, with the relief valve discharging into the pan, can satisfy the policy.

If your heater sits over a finished basement room or a closet over bedrooms, ask the plumber which approach the quote assumes.

Who pulls the permit.

  1. Mass.gov states that only a licensed master or journeyman plumber can do plumbing work and that permits are issued only to licensed plumbers..
  2. The plumber applies through the Dedham Building Department's Plumbing/Gas Division, which uses its OpenGov online portal..
  3. The work goes ahead once the permit is in place..
  4. The plumber arranges the inspection and fixes anything the inspector flags..
  5. Keep a copy of the permit record with your house papers for a future sale..

What happens on install day.

The plumber confirms the plan, shuts off the old unit, and sets the new tank on a stable base or hangs a tankless unit on its bracket. Connections follow: water lines with shutoff valves, then gas, venting or the electrical feed. The relief valve discharge pipe and any pan are piped next, and then the unit is filled, purged of air and fired.

Plan for the water to be off for part of the day, and keep the area clear. If you are not changing fuel or location, the job is usually the shortest version of this. If you are, expect extra trades such as an electrician, or a longer visit for gas piping.

Hard water and where the tank sits.

The Dedham-Westwood Water District reported average hardness of about 6.0 grains per gallon in 2025. Scale settles in any heater, so ask for a drain valve you can reach and a clear space around the tank for future flushes. A heater squeezed into a corner is harder to service, and servicing is what keeps scale from becoming a failure.

What an installation quote should list.

  • The heater, by fuel and tank size.
  • New venting, gas line, electrical circuit or condensate drain.
  • Code items: relief valve, discharge pipe, expansion tank, pan or shut-off device.
  • Permit, inspection and disposal of the old unit.
  • Access, such as stairs, tight closets or long pipe runs.

Codes, standards and sources.

Plumbing work in Massachusetts is governed by 248 CMR 10.00, the Uniform State Plumbing Code, and plumbers are licensed by the Board of State Examiners of Plumbers and Gas Fitters. You can read the state's consumer fact sheet on plumbers and gas fitters before you hire anyone.
