Water heater repair means a licensed plumber finds the failed part, such as an element, thermostat, burner or valve, and replaces it when that makes sense.

If the tank shell itself has failed, you will hear that straight, because no part fixes a rusted-through tank.

Water heater leaking right now: find the source first.

A leak is the symptom that sends most people to the phone, and where the water appears tells you a lot. Drips from the inlet and outlet fittings on top, or from the drain valve near the bottom, are usually repairable.

A steady stream from the discharge pipe beside the relief valve points to a valve or pressure problem. Water seeping from the base of the tank body usually means the inner steel has corroded through.

Dry the tank with a towel and watch where the first bead forms. Note whether the water is hot or cold, then take a photo for the plumber. If the leak is slow, a bucket and the shutoff valve buy you time. If it is fast or sits above finished rooms, shut the water off at once and call.

Warning signs before a full failure.

  • Rumbling, popping or ticking from the tank during a heating cycle.
  • Water that runs lukewarm, then cold, a few minutes into a shower.
  • A pilot flame that keeps going out or burns yellow on a gas unit.
  • A breaker that trips each time the electric heater starts.
  • Rust-colored hot water, but clear cold water from the same faucet.
  • Damp patches, rust streaks or mineral crust around fittings and the base.

Hard water and what it does to your tank.

The Dedham-Westwood Water District reported average hardness of about 6.0 grains per gallon in its 2025 water quality report, with samples ranging from 4.3 to 7.8. That is moderately hard water, mostly calcium and magnesium, and it leaves scale on electric elements and gas tank bottoms as it heats.

Scale insulates the heating surface, so elements burn out sooner, burners run longer and the tank rumbles. Ask the plumber whether flushing the tank makes sense on your unit, because a flush on a badly scaled tank sometimes exposes a leak that sediment was plugging.

How a plumber diagnoses a heater that has gone cold.

Diagnosis starts with what the unit does and does not do. On an electric heater, the plumber checks power at the panel and at the unit, then tests each element and thermostat with a meter. On a gas heater, the checks run to the gas control, thermocouple or igniter, and venting. A leaking tank gets a moisture check at every fitting to separate a loose connection from a failed shell.

There is a limit to what anyone can see. The inside of a steel tank cannot be inspected without cutting it open, so a plumber judges its condition from age, scale, rust at the fittings and how the leak behaves. Ask what the plumber found, show you the failed part and explain why repair or replacement is the better call.

Gas, electric and hybrid heater repair.

Gas tank heaters.

Common faults include a dirty or failed pilot assembly, a bad thermocouple, a gas control valve and a blocked flue. Any gas smell means you leave the building, call 911 from outside, then call your gas utility. Do not flip switches or hunt for the source yourself.

Electric tank heaters.

Upper and lower elements, thermostats and the high-limit reset switch cause most failures. No hot water at all often means the upper element or thermostat. Hot water that runs out fast often means the lower element.

Hybrid heat pump heaters.

These add a compressor, fan, condensate drain and sensors to a standard tank. Many faults are simple, such as a clogged air filter or a blocked condensate line. Ask the plumber up front whether they service hybrid units, since the control boards and refrigerant circuit are not the same as a basic tank.

Relief valve and drain pipe rules on a Massachusetts heater.

The Massachusetts plumbing code, 248 CMR 10.14(6)(f), requires a temperature and pressure relief valve on water heaters capable of exceeding 212 F. The discharge pipe must be full size, non-ferrous, with no shut-off valve, ending at a fixture or within 12 inches of the floor.

Tank-type heaters also need a vacuum relief valve. If your valve drips or its pipe has been capped or reduced, a plumber should correct it, because a blocked relief path is a safety fault rather than a repair to put off.

No hot water at all.

When the whole house goes cold, the cause is often a tripped breaker, a failed element or a pilot that has gone out. Check the breaker and the reset button before calling, then check that the gas valve is open on a gas unit.

If the heater is fine and one faucet is cold, the problem is probably a mixing valve or cartridge at that fixture, not the tank. Our faucet repair service covers those fixtures.

While you wait for the plumber.

  1. Close the cold water supply valve on top of the tank..
  2. Switch off the breaker for an electric heater, or set a gas control to pilot or off if you can reach it safely..
  3. Move boxes and stored items away from the base of the tank..
  4. Open one hot tap upstairs so pressure does not build if the valve is leaking..
  5. Photograph the tank, the label and the water so the plumber sees the problem right away..
  6. If a gas smell appears, leave the building and call 911 from outside..
