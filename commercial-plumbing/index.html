Who owns the problem: landlord, tenant or manager.

Most commercial calls stall on one question: whose pipe is it? The answer depends on the lease, so read it before you call, but do not delay the shutoff. Stop the water first and sort out responsibility after.

Backflow testing and the DWWD Cross Connection Control Program.

The Dedham-Westwood Water District runs a Cross Connection Control Program mandated by MassDEP. The District surveys commercial, industrial, institutional and municipal sites, and the backflow devices it identifies must be tested every six months as a fee-for-service. When a device fails, repairs are done by independent firms at the owner's expense. That is where a licensed plumber comes in.

For you, a failed test is a deadline, not a nuisance. Ask the plumber if the repair needs a permit, since code sets conditions for reduced-pressure-zone devices. Keep your test records together, and ask DWWD (781-329-7090) how to schedule a retest after repair. The District also recommends hose-bib vacuum breakers on hose connections, a cheap fix worth doing at any site with outdoor spigots.

Grease traps and kitchen drains.

A grease trap catches fat before it leaves the kitchen. When it fills, the drain slows, smells rise from floor sinks and the line backs up at the busiest time. We found no Dedham rule on pumping schedules, so we cannot cite one. Ask your landlord, your insurer and the Town engineering office whether a requirement applies to your building.

For service, ask the plumber what gets removed, how the trap is cleaned and where the waste goes. A kitchen that cleans on a regular cycle, rather than after a failure, saves the cost of a Saturday backup. If the kitchen drain behind the trap keeps clogging, see hydro jetting for the line itself.

Storm drains and roof drains.

Large lots shed rain into catch basins, and leaves and silt clog the grates. The Town runs separate sanitary and stormwater systems, so a lot drain on private property must go to stormwater and never to the sanitary sewer. Boston-area annual precipitation averages 43.59 inches (NOAA 1991-2020, Boston Logan), enough that a blocked drain turns into ponding.

Ask us to clear basin grates, roof leaders and trench drains before the wet months, and to flush them to check flow.

Commercial leak detection, honestly.

Leak detection in a commercial building is useful when the water bill climbs, a ceiling stains below a roof or riser, or a slab stays damp. It uses acoustic listening, thermal imaging and pressure tests to narrow down a leak, and it can narrow it down a lot, but it cannot see through everything.

Metal, concrete and noise limit results. A typical approach is to isolate zones by closing valves, then listen. For more detail, see leak detection.

Repair options and honest trade-offs.

  • Repair in place: fastest and cheapest when the pipe is sound outside the damaged section..
  • Replace a run: sensible when corrosion appears along the whole pipe..
  • Reroute: worth it when a pipe sits in an inaccessible chase..
  • Temporary fix tonight, permanent repair next week: reasonable when a business must open in the morning, as long as everyone knows it is temporary..
