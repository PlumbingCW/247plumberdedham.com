We answer one 24/7 line for all five towns. A town page is not a different company. It explains what is specific to that place: the water supplier, the age of the housing, the drainage trouble spots and the permit office.

Home base: Dedham.

Dedham is where dispatch starts. Neighborhoods people ask about most are Dedham Square, Oakdale, Riverdale, Greenlodge and East Dedham. Riverdale has the largest share of the town's Flood Plain Overlay District, and Manor, near Greenlodge and Oakdale, is described in a state study as flood prone, so basement water is a recurring emergency. Start on the homepage or browse all plumbing services.

Nearby towns.

Westwood shares the Dedham-Westwood Water District with Dedham, so water quality and the after-hours water line are the same. Needham has its own town water division and two ZIP codes. Norwood buys its water from the MWRA and sits along Route 1. Walpole runs its own wells and is the farthest of the four.

If your town is not listed.

Call the 24/7 line and give your address. The dispatcher will tell you whether a plumber can reach you. We do not promise coverage outside the five towns above, and we do not promise a fixed arrival time anywhere.

What changes from town to town.

Water hardness, sewer ownership, housing age and permit offices differ across the five towns, and those differences change the repair. A hard-water town ages water heaters faster. A town with older clay or cast iron laterals sees more root intrusion.

A town with a high water table needs sump pumps that work in March, not just in August. Each town page covers the details that apply there, so read the one that matches your address before you call.

For problems that are not urgent, ask about scheduling when you phone in. Planned jobs such as repiping, a sewer camera inspection or a water heater installation can be booked for a day that suits you.

How dispatch decides who goes where.

The dispatcher asks what is happening and books the next available plumber. Active flooding, sewage in living space and no heat in winter are the urgent cases, and planned work can wait for a convenient day. We never promise a fixed arrival time for any town.

You will hear an honest window on the phone, and you can call (617) 752-0573 any hour to get one.

Questions people ask about coverage.

If you live between two towns, the address decides, not the mailing name. Needham has two ZIP codes, 02492 and 02494, and Norwood and Westwood each use one. Walpole is served from Dedham but is the longest trip, so expect the dispatcher to be upfront about timing. Businesses along Route 1 in Norwood and Dedham can reach the same line, and the commercial plumbing page explains after-hours work for shops and offices.

Where to go next.

Pick the page for your town, or use plumbing services to find the service that matches your problem. The contact page lists the business address, and the homepage gives the short version. Call now if water is already running.

Before you call about a town page.

Have the street address, the nearest cross street and a one-line description of the problem ready. If water is running, shut off the main valve first when you can reach it safely. If the floor is wet and electrical equipment is nearby, stay away from it and say so on the call.

These steps help the dispatcher decide how fast to move and what the plumber should bring, whichever of the five towns you live in. The blog has longer guides on frozen pipes, sump pumps and water heaters if you want background while you wait.

A note on distance.

Dedham to Westwood and Needham is a short drive on local roads. Norwood is reached along Route 1 or Route 1A, and Walpole is farther south. Traffic on Route 1 at rush hour can double a short trip, so late-afternoon calls to the outer towns may take longer than a call at midnight.

The dispatcher will tell you what to expect when you phone, and will not quote a time the schedule cannot meet.
