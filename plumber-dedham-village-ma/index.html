Dedham Village is the historic center of the town. The master plan describes a settlement that dates to the 1600s, a Norfolk County seat since 1793 and a core that keeps much of its historic character. Residential streets sit next to commercial buildings around Dedham Square, so this page keeps home plumbing and business plumbing in separate sections.

What Dedham Village is, from the Town's records.

The master plan lists Dedham Village (Dedham Square) as one of six neighborhoods. It covers about 315 acres and roughly 590 housing units. The Dedham Village Historic District is listed on the National Register (2006) and covers 55 acres and 342 properties. Three local historic districts sit within it: Connecticut Corner, Franklin Square-Court Street and Federal Hill.

Landmarks named by the Town include the Norfolk County Courthouse on High Street (1827) and Dedham Common. The Town's survey counts 16 to 20 one- and two-story commercial buildings in Dedham Square dating from the 1790s to the 1940s. The plan describes grand single-family residences in the area. No boundary line is published, so this page does not draw one.

Plumbing in a historic house.

An older house in a historic district often has additions and updated systems layered over the original. The Town's records describe the architecture, not the pipes, so nothing here claims a material or a failure rate. A plumber who inspects the system will tell you what is there.

If your house sits in a local historic district, ask the Town's Historic Districts Commission whether exterior changes need review before outside work such as venting or new penetrations. Interior plumbing questions are for a licensed plumber. Common calls are leak detection, water heater replacement and drain cleaning.

Plumbing for a business near Dedham Square.

A restaurant, office or shop in an older commercial building has its own questions: after-hours repairs, floor drains, restrooms and backflow. The Dedham-Westwood Water District runs a cross connection control program under state rules. It surveys commercial, industrial, institutional and municipal sites, and requires backflow devices to be tested every six months, with repairs by independent firms at the owner's expense.

If you manage a building here, ask for commercial plumbing help and confirm who tests your backflow device. Tell the plumber what the building is used for when you call.

Codes, standards and sources.

Plumbing work in Massachusetts is governed by 248 CMR 10.00, the Uniform State Plumbing Code, and plumbers are licensed by the Board of State Examiners of Plumbers and Gas Fitters. You can read the state's consumer fact sheet on plumbers and gas fitters before you hire anyone.
