While you wait, stop using every fixture on that drain line and keep the washing machine off. Pull a towel around the base of the tub or floor drain, and lift anything valuable off the floor.

Kitchen sink clogs: grease, food and the slow drain that finally quits.

Most calls we hear start in the kitchen. A sink that drains slowly for weeks, gurgles when the dishwasher runs, then stops completely is a classic grease and food-solids clog. Cooking fat cools inside the pipe and coats the walls, and every rinse adds another layer.

Cabling a kitchen line means feeding a small drum machine through the trap and breaking the blockage apart, then pulling the debris back out. If the cable passes through easily but the sink slows again within weeks, the pipe wall probably still carries a coat of grease. That is the moment to ask about hydro jetting, which washes the wall instead of poking a hole.

A garbage disposal that hums and will not clear is a separate repair. We cover it under garbage disposal repair.

How to tell a single clog from a main line problem.

Where the water shows up tells you most of what a plumber will find. One slow fixture usually means a clog in that fixture's own branch. Several fixtures acting up together point to the main drain.

  • One sink or tub drains slowly while every other drain works normally.
  • Gurgling from a drain when another fixture runs or a toilet flushes.
  • Water backing into the tub or shower when the washing machine empties.
  • A floor drain in the basement that bubbles or pools after heavy water use.
  • Every drain slow at the same time, or a toilet that bubbles when you run the sink.
  • A sewer smell in the basement that comes and goes with water use.

If you see the last three signs, treat it as a main line problem and read our page on sewer line cleaning. Cleaning one fixture will not fix a blocked main.

Why older Dedham houses clog more often.

About 74 percent of Dedham homes were built before 1978, according to the town's Master Plan. That matters because older houses often carry a mix of drain materials from different decades, with long horizontal runs, worn joints and rough inner walls where grease and hair catch. When your plumber cables a line, ask what the cable felt like at each bend and whether a camera is worth running before the next clog.

The neighborhoods vary. The Master Plan describes the Oakdale area as mostly 1950s capes, split-levels and ranches, and Mother Brook's mill-worker streets as historic housing. A single-level ranch and a multi-story house with a long vertical stack clog in different places, so tell the dispatcher your house type and which floor the problem is on.

Snaking, cabling and when we step up to jetting.

These words get used loosely, so here is how the methods compare in practice.

Method Best for Limit.
Hand auger Hair and soap in a tub, shower or bathroom sink Short reach, makes a hole in the clog only.
Drum machine cable Kitchen lines, laundry lines, most branch lines Can leave a grease coat on the wall.
Larger cable with cutting head Roots or hardened buildup in bigger drains Not for fragile or collapsed pipe.
Hydro jetting Grease, scale and repeat clogs across the full pipe wall Needs access, wrong tool for broken pipe.

Cabling is the right first move for most clogs because it is quick and cheap. If the same drain clogs again inside a few months, the method was probably not the problem. The pipe was.

Drain line leaks and bad slopes.

Sometimes a drain that keeps clogging is also leaking, or sagging so water sits in a low spot and collects debris. Signs include a damp patch on a basement ceiling under a bathroom, a musty smell near the stack, or a clog that returns in the same place every time. A camera run shows the sag or crack. We then talk through a spot repair, which may need a permit.

In Massachusetts, clearing a blocked drain is a minor repair that needs no permit, but replacing piping does, and plumbing permits are issued only to licensed plumbers. Ask any contractor for the license type and number, and check it yourself on the Mass.gov license lookup before they cut into a wall.

What drain cleaning costs and what changes the number.

Price depends on the job, not the day on the calendar. Five things move the quote:

  • Which drain: a bathroom sink branch is shorter than a main line with a distant clog.
  • Access: a visible cleanout is quick, while a pulled toilet or removed trap adds labor.
  • Equipment: hand auger, drum cable and jetter are different tools with different time on site.
  • After-hours timing: nights and weekends carry a different rate, which you should hear before work starts.
  • Repeat visits: a clog from the pipe's condition may need a camera or jetting, not another cable run.

What to do while you wait for the plumber.

  1. Stop all water use on that line, including the washing machine and dishwasher..
  2. Do not pour chemical drain cleaner into standing water. It sits in the pipe and burns skin and tools when we open the line..
  3. Find the basement cleanout cap if you have one, and clear the area around it..
  4. Note which fixtures are slow and which still work, because that tells us where the clog sits..
  5. Move rugs, boxes and electronics away from floor drains..
  6. If sewage is rising from a floor drain, keep children and pets away and say so when you call..
