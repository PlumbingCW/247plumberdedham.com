40 Gallon Power Vent Water Heater Install Cost in Dedham, MA

A power vent tank costs more to install than a standard atmospheric one because of its fan, its plastic vent and its need for power. Here is how each piece shows up in a quote.

The 40 gallon power vent water heater install cost in Dedham, MA depends less on the tank than on everything around it. A power vent heater is a storage tank with a blower on top that pushes exhaust gases out through a sealed pipe, usually PVC, instead of relying on a hot chimney draft. A 40 gallon size serves many households.

The install price changes with the vent route, the electrical outlet, the permit and what must be removed. We list cost drivers here and no dollar figures, because a number from a chart will not match your basement.

How a power vent heater works.

In a standard gas tank, hot exhaust rises on its own through a metal flue into a chimney. A power vent unit adds an electric blower that forces exhaust out through plastic pipe, which can run through a side wall or up through the roof. That frees the plumber from needing a masonry chimney.

The blower needs power, which is why the heater must be near an outlet. It also uses a condensate or intake arrangement that depends on the model, and the vent pipe must follow the manufacturer's rules for length, slope and elbows.

That extra equipment explains the price gap. It also means more parts that can eventually fail, like the blower motor and pressure switch.

The cost drivers, in order of impact.

  1. Venting route. A short run through a nearby exterior wall is simple. A long route, a roof penetration or a path through finished ceilings adds labor and materials..
  2. Electrical supply. The blower needs a grounded outlet. If none exists nearby, a licensed electrician has to add one, which is separate work..
  3. Existing setup. Swapping a power vent for another power vent is quicker. Converting from an old atmospheric heater means abandoning the old flue and closing it properly..
  4. Gas or fuel connection. Old gas flex lines, valves and drip legs may need replacement to meet code..
  5. Code items. The relief valve discharge pipe, a vacuum relief valve and, in some locations, a safe waste pan..
  6. Permit and inspection. A licensed plumber pulls the permit, and the fee is part of the job..
  7. Old-tank removal and disposal, including carrying it out of a basement..
  8. Access. Tight closets, steep stairs and finished spaces take more time..

Choosing a power vent over other types.

Power vent makes sense when there is no usable chimney, when you want flexibility in where the heater sits, or when the old flue is in poor shape. It may not be the best choice if the outlet or the vent run is awkward.

In that case, a direct vent or an electric heat pump model might fit better, and a plumber can compare them. Our water heater replacement page describes the broader tank-versus-other decision.

If you are weighing a bigger switch like tankless, remember it needs a different gas line and vent, and it will price differently. We keep that discussion on its own page.

What Dedham homeowners should know.

Under 248 CMR 10.14, which the Mass.gov plumbing code document covers, a water heater capable of exceeding 212 degrees Fahrenheit needs a temperature and pressure relief valve. The discharge must be full size, non-ferrous, without a shutoff, and end at a fixture or within 12 inches of the floor. Tank-type heaters also need a vacuum relief valve. These items are in every honest quote, and they are not optional extras.

The safe waste pan policy from Mass.gov applies to heaters and boilers of 6 gallons or more above an occupied space. A 40 gallon tank qualifies by size. If your heater sits above finished living area, expect either a pan piped to a trapped fixture or within 12 inches of the floor, or the replacement exception with an approved automatic shutoff device plus a pan. Ask which one is planned.

The Mass.gov consumer fact sheet states permits for plumbing work are issued only to licensed plumbers, and the Dedham Building Department has a Plumbing/Gas Division. The permit and its inspection protect you if you sell or file an insurance claim later.

The Dedham-Westwood Water District's 2025 water quality report gives average hardness of 6.0 grains per gallon. For a storage tank, that means sediment accumulates at the bottom over the years. Ask the installer what flushing schedule fits the model.

Vent safety and what to do if you smell fumes.

A power vent heater sends exhaust outdoors, but a poorly sealed joint or blocked termination can let gas leak into the house. After any installation, ask the plumber to show you the vent termination outside and confirm it is clear of windows, dryer vents and snow drifts. Keep CO alarms working on every level.

If you smell gas, leave the building and call 911 from outside, then call your gas utility. Mass.gov advises not to flip switches or try to shut off gas yourself.

Comparing bids without being fooled.

Make sure each bid names the same heater capacity, fuel and venting plan. A very low quote often leaves out the outlet, vent changes, permit or haul-away. A high one may include upgrades you do not need. Ask each plumber to walk the vent path with you, then compare the final line items.
