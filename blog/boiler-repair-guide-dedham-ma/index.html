Boiler Repair Guide for Dedham, MA: Checks Before You Call

A cold house in January is stressful. These checks tell you what you can safely look at, and where to stop and hand the boiler to a licensed plumber.

This boiler repair guide for Dedham, MA is built around one question: the house is cold, so what can you safely check before calling? A boiler is a sealed heating appliance that warms water (or makes steam) and sends it to radiators, baseboard or in-floor loops.

Most no-heat calls trace back to a short list of causes, and several of them take two minutes to rule out. Others involve gas, combustion and pressure, and those belong to a licensed professional.

Read this first: gas smell and carbon monoxide.

If you smell gas inside, leave the building right away. Call 911 from outside, then call your gas utility. The Mass.gov gas safety guidance says not to turn lights or appliances on or off, not to open or close windows, and not to try to shut off the gas yourself. Do not go back in to look for the source.

Carbon monoxide has no smell. Any fuel-burning boiler can produce it if venting or combustion goes wrong. Working CO alarms on every level and outside bedrooms are the practical defense. If an alarm sounds, or you feel headache, dizziness or nausea that eases when you go outdoors, get everyone out and call 911.

Never relight a boiler that has repeatedly shut down, and never bypass a safety control to make it run.

Five-minute no-heat triage.

  1. Check the thermostat. Confirm it is set to heat, the setpoint is above the room temperature, and the batteries are fresh..
  2. Check power. Look for a tripped breaker and the boiler's own service switch, which often looks like a light switch near the unit..
  3. Check fuel. If you have an oil tank, read the gauge. For gas, other gas appliances like the stove burner can tell you whether gas is reaching the house. If neither works, call your gas utility..
  4. Read the pressure gauge. Most residential hot-water systems sit in a modest range when cold, and the manual or label on your boiler gives the exact number. A reading near zero suggests a leak or lost water..
  5. Look for a lockout light or error code and write it down for the plumber..

If all five look normal and the boiler still will not fire, stop. Do not open the burner or combustion area.

Banging or kettle-like noise Scale, low water or a failing pump Turn it off and call.

Pressure climbs high or relief valve drips Expansion tank or fill valve trouble Turn it off and call.

Bleeding a radiator: what is reasonable.

If radiators are cold at the top and warm at the bottom, trapped air is the usual reason. On a hot-water system with radiators, a homeowner can often bleed them with a radiator key. Turn the system off first. Open the bleed valve a quarter turn with a rag held below it, close it when water appears, and check the gauge afterward.

Do not do this on a steam system. Steam radiators use different valves and the water is hot enough to scald. If you are not sure which type you have, look at the pipes and the manual, or ask the plumber. When the gauge drops after bleeding, the system may need water added. If you do not know how to add it safely, wait for a technician.

When to stop and call.

Call a licensed plumber when the boiler smells of fuel, shows soot or scorch marks, trips its safety lockout repeatedly, leaks, makes banging sounds, or loses pressure again after you refill it. The same goes for any repair that needs the burner, gas valve, venting or electrical controls opened. These are not casual jobs, and Massachusetts reserves plumbing and gas fitting work to licensed professionals.

Many older New England homes heat with hot-water boilers, though we do not have a Dedham figure for how many. Because the details vary house by house, a plumber should look at the actual equipment. What Dedham homeowners should know.

NOAA's 1991 to 2020 climate normals for the Boston area (the Logan Airport station, not a Dedham station) put the January average low at 23.1 degrees Fahrenheit and February at 24.6. That is a long run of nights where a dead boiler becomes a real problem within hours.

If the house starts to cool, open interior doors, close off unused rooms and keep the warmest room in use while you wait for repair.

The Master Plan "Designing Dedham 2035" reports that 74 percent of Dedham houses were built before 1978. For you, that means the heating equipment and the piping around it may be older than the boiler itself, so a technician should look at valves, supply lines and the condition of old connections while on site.

Two code points apply to boiler work. Mass.gov states that permits for plumbing work are issued only to licensed plumbers. The safe waste pan policy applies to boilers of 6 gallons or more installed above an occupied space, with the pan piped to a trapped fixture or within 12 inches of the floor. If your boiler is being replaced, ask the plumber how the pan and permit are handled.

Keep the house safe while you wait.

  • Close the doors of rooms you do not need and keep the family in one room.
  • Never use a gas oven, stove burner or outdoor grill to heat the home, because they produce carbon monoxide.
  • If rooms get cold enough that pipes could freeze, ask the plumber on the phone whether to shut off the water and drain the lines.
  • Check on older neighbors and relatives who may not tell you they are cold.
  • Keep the boiler area clear of boxes and chemicals.

If pipes do freeze, our leak detection page covers finding where water is escaping after a thaw.

Codes, standards and sources.

Plumbing work in Massachusetts is governed by 248 CMR 10.00, the Uniform State Plumbing Code, and plumbers are licensed by the Board of State Examiners of Plumbers and Gas Fitters. You can read the state's consumer fact sheet on plumbers and gas fitters before you hire anyone.
