DIY Tankless Water Heater Install in Dedham, MA: Honest Answer

Tankless units look simple on the wall. The gas, the vent and the permit are why this one belongs to a licensed plumber in Massachusetts.

A DIY tankless water heater install in Dedham, MA is not something the state lets a homeowner do on their own. A tankless water heater heats water on demand as it passes through a burner or element, instead of storing it in a tank.

Under 248 CMR 10.00, the Uniform State Plumbing Code, installing, altering or replacing plumbing is regulated work. The Mass.gov consumer fact sheet says only a licensed plumber may do plumbing work and that permits are issued only to licensed plumbers.

You can still do real work around the heater. Flushing scale and cleaning the inlet filter are the two jobs a homeowner can handle, and they matter in this area.

Why the install is a licensed job.

Three systems meet at a tankless unit, and each one can hurt someone if it is done wrong.

Gas first. Most gas tankless heaters need far more fuel flow than a tank heater does when they fire. The gas line, meter capacity and fittings must be checked and sometimes enlarged. A bad gas connection is a leak, and Mass.gov's guidance on gas smell is to leave the building and call 911 from outside.

Venting second. A condensing tankless unit uses sealed vent pipe made for its exhaust. Wrong materials, wrong slope or a missing condensate path can push exhaust gases, including carbon monoxide, back into the home.

Electrical third. Even a gas tankless heater needs power for controls and fans. Electric tankless units draw a very large load, which can mean new breakers, heavier wire and sometimes a service upgrade.

On top of those, the permit rule decides the question. Mass.gov states that a local plumbing inspector must issue a permit before plumbing is installed, altered, removed, replaced or repaired. If a homeowner cannot get the permit, an inspection cannot happen, and unpermitted gas work can create trouble with insurance and at sale.

What a homeowner can do: flush and filter.

  1. Read the manual for your exact model. Some manufacturers specify the acid or descaler, the flush time and the isolation valves..
  2. Turn off the power or put the gas control in its off setting, and close the cold and hot isolation valves..
  3. Connect hoses to the service ports and run a descaling solution through the unit in a loop for the time the manufacturer lists..
  4. Rinse with clean water until it runs clear, and drain the hoses into a bucket, not onto the floor..
  5. Remove, clean and replace the inlet screen, which catches grit and flakes before they reach the heat exchanger..
  6. Reopen the valves slowly, check for drips, and restore power or gas as the manual directs..

If your unit has no isolation valves, the descale job is harder and a plumber should add them. If you smell gas at any point, stop and follow the safety steps above. Do not try to fix a gas leak yourself.

What Dedham homeowners should know.

The Dedham-Westwood Water District's 2025 water quality report gives hardness at 4.3 to 7.8 grains per gallon, with an average of 6.0. It is mostly calcium and magnesium. That is why tankless descaling comes up in this area: scale narrows the passages in the heat exchanger and cuts flow.

What does it mean for your decision? Whichever unit you buy, make the flush part of the plan. Ask the plumber to install isolation valves with service ports so the flush is easy, and ask how often the manufacturer wants it done in water of this hardness.

For the permit, Dedham's Building Department has a Plumbing/Gas Division. The Building Department takes permit applications through its online portal. Ask your plumber to confirm the permit is filed, and request a copy for your records.

Gas, venting and sizing at a general level.

You do not need the engineering, but you should know the questions.

Gas: A tankless heater has a maximum input rating. The plumber compares that rating with the existing pipe diameter and length, and with every other gas appliance in the house. If the line is too small, it gets upsized.

Venting: Plumber and manufacturer instructions set the vent material, the length limit and how many elbows are allowed. Longer or more complicated runs cost more. The outlet location must also stay clear of windows, other vents and snow.

Flow: The unit is sized by how many fixtures run at once and how much the incoming water must be heated. Colder winter water means a lower flow rate at the same temperature setting, so the unit should be sized for January, not July.

Questions to ask each bidder.

  • Is the permit included, and who schedules the inspection?.
  • Will the gas line be resized, and is that line item separate?.
  • What vent material and route are planned, and where does the exhaust exit?.
  • Are isolation valves and service ports included for flushing?.
  • How is the condensate drained and neutralized?.
  • What do the warranty terms say about maintenance and hard water?.
