How to Hydro Jet a Sewer Line in Dedham, MA (and When Not To)

Hydro jetting scours a pipe with high-pressure water. Done right it clears buildup a snake leaves behind. Done wrong it can hurt people and pipes.

If you want to know how to hydro jet a sewer line in Dedham, MA, the honest answer starts with what jetting is and who should run it. Hydro jetting pushes a hose with a nozzle through a drain line while water at very high pressure blasts from jets that point backward and sideways.

The jets pull the hose forward and scrape grease, scale and soft debris off the pipe wall. A professional does this only after learning what the pipe looks like inside. For most homeowners, the right move is to call a licensed plumber rather than rent a machine.

What hydro jetting does that a snake does not.

A drain snake, also called a cable, bores a hole through a blockage. The cable pushes through, the water drains, and the pipe wall may still be coated with grease or scale. The clog returns in weeks because the opening is only a little wider than the cable.

A jetter works differently. The water stream cuts and flushes the buildup, so the pipe gets closer to its full diameter. That is why jetting is often recommended for repeat grease clogs and heavy sludge. It can also cut through softer root growth, although roots come back unless the underlying crack is repaired.

A snake can fail for several reasons. The cable can stop at a bend, catch on a root mass and spin without clearing it, or poke a hole through the middle of a heavy clog while the wall buildup stays. Callers often describe a snake that got stuck or a clog that returned after a few weeks, and both are signs that something more than a soft clog is in the line.

Why a camera comes first.

Jetting assumes a pipe that can take pressure. If the pipe is cracked, offset at a joint, collapsed, or has a section with a belly, the high-pressure stream can make things worse. Water may blast out through a crack and wash soil away from the pipe. A nozzle can also snag on a broken edge.

That is why a camera inspection should come before jetting on any line you have not seen. Ask whether the plumber brings a camera and whether you will see the video. Our sewer inspection page explains what a camera shows and what it cannot.

Why a rental jetter is risky.

Rental yards sell the idea that this is a weekend job. The hazards are real.

  • The nozzle exits the hose at pressure strong enough to cut skin. A hose that whips out of the cleanout can injure you before you react..
  • A line that is blocked ahead of the nozzle may build pressure and force sewage and water back out through the cleanout or the lowest drain in the house..
  • Using too much pressure on thin, old or damaged pipe can crack it..
  • An unknown connection, like a sump pump or a roof drain, can send water to places you did not intend..
  • Sewage carries bacteria. Spray from a cleanout is a health hazard in an enclosed basement..
  • You rarely have a way to see what happened inside the pipe afterward, so you may not know if you made a crack..

A professional machine is a different tool, with pressure ratings, nozzle choices and control valves a trained operator uses. Even then, the plumber matches the nozzle and pressure to the pipe, and stops if the camera shows damage.

What Dedham homeowners should know.

The Town of Dedham maintains the sanitary sewer, according to the Town's Engineering Department. The sewage flows to MWRA interceptors and then to the Deer Island plant. Your private line connects to that public sewer, so what you do upstream moves downstream.

The Town also reported sewage backups in the Manor area and tied them to clean water entering the sewer. Its Door-to-Door Inspection Program, which covered about 1,600 properties in Manor and parts of Oakdale and Greenlodge, looked for private connections like sump pumps and exterior roof drains tied to the sanitary sewer.

So here is the rule of thumb. Never jet toward a connection you cannot identify. If you jet a line that also carries a sump discharge or a roof drain, pressure and water volume can behave unpredictably. A plumber with a camera can tell you what actually enters the line, and the Town states that discharging clean rain or groundwater into the sanitary sewer is prohibited under federal, state and local law.

What to ask a plumber before a jetting job.

  • Will you run a camera before and after the jet?.
  • What nozzle and pressure range suits my pipe material and age?.
  • Where will the equipment connect, and how will you protect my floors?.
  • What happens if the camera shows a crack or an offset joint?.
  • Is the cleanout accessible, or is access part of the quote?.
  • What do I do about the roots that cause repeat blockages?.

Clearing a drain is a minor repair that needs no permit under the Mass.gov consumer fact sheet. Replacing or relocating piping does, so a repair after jetting can bring a permit into play.
