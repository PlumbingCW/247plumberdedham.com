Sink Not Draining but Not Clogged in Dedham, MA

If the plunger and the drain cleaner did nothing, the blockage may not be in the sink pipe at all. Look at vents, traps, air gaps and the main line.

A sink not draining but not clogged in Dedham, MA usually has one of five causes: a blocked plumbing vent, a partly blocked trap, a kinked or clogged dishwasher or disposal connection, a failed air gap, or a restriction farther down the line. The sink is simply where you see it first.

A drain that has stopped completely is a different problem from one that empties slowly and gurgles, so your first job is to watch exactly how the water behaves.

Read the symptoms before you touch a wrench.

Drain empties, then stands again in a minute Partial clog just past the trap Remove and clean the trap.

Sink slow only when dishwasher runs Shared drain restriction Disposal knockout plug, hose.
Tub, toilet and sinks all slow Main line Stop and call.
Sewer smell with slow drain Dry trap, vent or main line Run water, then call if it persists.

Gurgling is the clue most people miss. When a drain pipe empties, air must come in behind the water. If the vent cannot supply it, the water drags air through the trap and makes that bubbling sound. A drain that gurgles but shows no hair, grease or food is a vent suspect.

Trap and tailpiece: the five-minute check.

Put a bucket under the sink. Unscrew the slip nuts on the trap by hand and slide it off. Dump the contents and rinse it. Look inside the pipe going into the wall with a flashlight. A thick coat of grease or soap film in the wall pipe is common, and it narrows the pipe even when no single clog is visible.

Reassemble with the washers seated flat. Run water for a minute and look for drips. If the sink still crawls, the restriction is farther down.

A plastic drain snake can reach a few feet into the wall. Push gently and do not force it. A metal cable can scratch old pipes and crack a trap arm. Stop if the cable meets firm resistance, because it may have reached a bend, a hub or a collapse.

The dishwasher and disposal connection.

Kitchen sinks share a drain with the dishwasher and often the disposal. The dishwasher drain hose should loop up high under the counter, or run through an air gap on the sink deck, before it joins the disposal or the tailpiece. If the loop is missing, wastewater from the sink can flow back into the dishwasher and make it smell.

A new disposal has a plastic knockout plug on the dishwasher inlet. If it was never punched out, the dishwasher cannot drain. If it was punched out but not cleaned, food can pack behind it.

An air gap is the small cap on the sink or counter. If it spits water or gurgles, it is clogged. Lift the cap, clear the tube and rinse. If water pours out of the gap, the line past it is blocked.

Never put your hand in a disposal. Turn it off at the switch and the breaker if you need to clear it. Use the hex key slot under the unit to free a stuck blade, and a pair of tongs to pull out items. A disposal that hums but does not turn, or leaks at the seams, needs garbage disposal repair.

When the main line is the problem.

If more than one fixture is slow, the restriction is likely downstream of all of them. Signs include:

  • A toilet that bubbles or a tub that gurgles when the sink drains..
  • Water appearing in a basement floor drain when you run a washer..
  • Slow drains on every floor..
  • A sewer smell that returns after you clean the trap..

This is not a sink problem. It is a main drain or building sewer problem, caused by roots, grease, scale, a belly in the pipe or a collapse. A plunger will not fix it, and chemical cleaners can make things worse by sitting in a pipe you cannot reach.

The right call is drain cleaning with a camera to see what you are dealing with. Ask whether the plumber brings a camera before the visit.

Stop immediately and call if sewage is coming up in a tub or floor drain, if you smell gas, or if the backup keeps returning. For gas, leave the building and call 911 from outside.

What Dedham homeowners should know.

The Town of Dedham maintains the public sanitary sewer, and the sewage flows to MWRA interceptors. That matters because a backup in several fixtures may be a private pipe problem on your lot or a problem in the Town's main. Ask the Town Engineering Department where its responsibility ends and yours begins. Call before you pay for a dig, since the answer changes who handles the repair.

The Town also reported sewage backups in the Manor neighborhood and tied them to clean water entering the sewer. Its door-to-door inspection program looked at sump pumps and roof drains connected to the sanitary line. If you live in an area like that, a pump or downspout draining to the sewer can overload the line during heavy rain. Say so when you call, because it helps the plumber decide where to look.

About 74 percent of Dedham homes were built before 1978, according to the Dedham Master Plan. Older houses often have older drain lines and vents that have collected decades of buildup. That does not mean yours is bad, but it is a reason to ask for a camera look if you have repeat problems.

Massachusetts treats clearing a blocked drain as a minor repair that does not need a permit. Replacing piping does, and the permit goes through a licensed plumber.

Quick fixes that are safe, and ones to skip.

Safe: hot water flushes, a baking soda and vinegar rinse, cleaning the trap and the air gap, and a small hand auger used gently.

Skip: caustic drain cleaners (they can damage pipes and burn skin), long power augers rented without experience, and any attempt to open a vent on a roof in winter without a harness.

If a vent is the issue, a roofer or plumber with proper fall protection should clear it. Do not climb onto an icy roof.
