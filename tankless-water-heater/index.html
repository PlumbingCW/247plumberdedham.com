Tankless Water Heater Service in Dedham, MA

Tankless units heat water on demand, and in moderately hard water the heat exchanger needs scheduled care. Licensed plumbers install, service, flush and descale them.

A tankless water heater in Dedham, MA heats water only when a tap opens, using a gas burner or electric elements around a compact heat exchanger. There is no storage tank to rust through, but there is a narrow heat exchanger, and mineral scale is its main enemy.

A licensed plumber can install a unit, diagnose error codes, repair parts and descale the exchanger. This page starts with the water, because the water decides how you maintain the unit.

What Dedham water does inside a tankless heat exchanger.

The Dedham-Westwood Water District reported average hardness of 6.0 grains per gallon in its 2025 water quality report, with samples ranging from 4.3 to 7.8 (74.1 to 134 ppm as calcium carbonate, sampled May 30, 2025). That is moderately hard water, mostly calcium and magnesium. The District also adjusts pH and adds orthophosphate for corrosion control, which protects pipes but does not remove hardness.

For a tankless unit, hardness matters because heat drives minerals out of the water as scale on the hottest surfaces. Scale narrows the flow channels, so the unit works harder, throws error codes or delivers weaker output. Treat flushing as routine upkeep in this water, not as an optional extra.

Tankless flush and descale, step by step.

Flushing circulates a descaling solution through the heat exchanger to dissolve mineral buildup, then rinses it clear. Follow the manufacturer's manual for the interval and solution, and know that harder water usually calls for more frequent service. A plumber will typically do the following:

  1. Shut off the gas or power and the water supply to the unit..
  2. Close the isolation valves and open the service valves on the hot and cold lines..
  3. Connect hoses and a small pump to circulate the descaling solution through the exchanger..
  4. Circulate for the time the manual specifies, then drain the solution..
  5. Rinse with clean water, clean the inlet filter screen and close the service ports..
  6. Restore power or gas, restart the unit and check the output and error history..

If your unit has no service valves, ask the plumber to add them. Without them, a proper flush is hard to do.

Signs your tankless heater needs attention.

  • An error code on the display that returns after a reset.
  • Hot water that cuts out or surges mid-shower, the so-called cold-water sandwich.
  • Lower flow than the unit gave when new.
  • Rumbling or whistling from the heat exchanger.
  • A fan or igniter that clicks without lighting on a gas unit.
  • A long delay before hot water arrives at a distant fixture.

When service becomes a repair.

A flush clears scale, but it cannot fix a failed part. Plumbers also replace igniters, flame sensors, flow sensors, fans, thermistors and control boards, and clear blocked inlet screens. If the exchanger itself is leaking or badly corroded, replacing the unit may cost less than the part.

If your tankless unit has stopped heating entirely, water heater repair covers the emergency steps, and a failed unit may lead to water heater replacement.

Gas line, venting and electrical needs.

A gas tankless heater can draw more gas at once than a typical tank burner, so ask whether the gas line needs upgrading. It also needs sealed venting and combustion air. An electric unit needs a heavy dedicated circuit and a panel with spare capacity. Any plumbing change like this needs a permit.

Mass.gov says permits are issued only to licensed plumbers, and in Dedham they go through the Building Department's Plumbing/Gas Division. If you smell gas at any time, leave the building, call 911 from outside, and call your gas utility.

Before the plumber arrives.

  • Write down the model, the error code and what you saw or heard.
  • Clear space around the unit and the isolation valves.
  • Note any change in water pressure at other fixtures.
  • Tell the plumber if the house has a water softener or filter, since that changes the scale picture.
  • Plan for the hot water to be off for part of the visit.
